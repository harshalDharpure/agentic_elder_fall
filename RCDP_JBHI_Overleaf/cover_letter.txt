Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We submit the manuscript "Confidence-Gated Dual-Process Fall Detection: Retrieval-Augmented Reasoning and Cost-Sensitive Response on Wearable Inertial Sensor Data" for consideration as a Regular Paper in the IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics.

Innovation. Wearable fall detectors are usually improved by retraining the detector. We take a different approach. We keep a validated detector frozen and add a decision layer around it. A two-threshold confidence gate, calibrated under an explicit missed-fall/false-alarm cost, sends only ambiguous windows to a deliberative path. That path combines serialised biomechanical evidence, retrieval of labelled training cases and a locally hosted language model. Its critic can refine severity and action but can never overturn the label. We give the decision-theoretic conditions under which deferral helps.

Results. Under the submission protocol, the selective decision layer achieved F1 0.772 +/- 0.025 versus 0.768 +/- 0.019 for the frozen detector, with expected cost 1079.6 +/- 115.3 versus 1086.6 +/- 80.6 across five subject-independent folds. Event-level and prospective validation remain future work.

Significance to JBHI. The work addresses wearable health monitoring, clinical decision support and trustworthy use of language models in health informatics. It gives monitoring services a retraining-free, privacy-preserving (fully local) way to reduce missed falls with an existing detector under scripted triage context. We report limits openly: latency suits offline caregiver triage rather than real-time alarms, and window-level labels require event-level and prospective validation.

Originality. This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by any other journal or conference. All authors have approved the submission. The study uses only public datasets (SisFall, KFall); no new human-subject data were collected. No related preprint or conference version exists.
